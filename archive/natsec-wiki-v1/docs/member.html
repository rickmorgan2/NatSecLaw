U.S. Representative for Colorado’s 6th District (CO-06)

Colorado’s 6th Congressional District. Democrat. Former Army Ranger and paratrooper with three combat tours in Iraq and Afghanistan. First elected in 2018 and reelected to a fifth term on November 3, 2026. In the 119th Congress he serves on the House Armed Services Committee and the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence. Committee assignments for the 120th Congress had not been announced when this page was last reviewed.

Committee jurisdiction

Each committee seat maps to a slice of the wiki. The lists below are the statutes most central to that committee’s work.

House Armed Services Committee

Role. Member. Ranking Member of the Subcommittee on Intelligence and Special Operations.

Jurisdiction. The Defense Department and the armed forces, which in code terms means Title 10 and the annual defense authorization. The subcommittee covers special operations forces, defense intelligence, counterterrorism, and related science and technology programs.

Core statutes. NDAA (annual), Title 10 U.S.C., Goldwater-Nichols Act, Nunn-Cohen Amendment, Section 127e, Section 394 (cyber), Security Cooperation (Ch. 16), Leahy Laws, Reserve & Guard Mobilization, UCMJ, Defense Production Act, War Powers Resolution, 2001 AUMF.

Code titles. Title 10, Title 32. Source: New America, member profile (committee roles).

House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence

Role. Member. Reported subcommittees are Central Intelligence Agency and Open Source Intelligence.

Jurisdiction. The intelligence community and the annual intelligence authorization, which in code terms means the intelligence chapters of Title 50. The committee receives covert action findings and oversees surveillance authorities.

Core statutes. Title 50 U.S.C., National Security Act, CIA Act, Intel Oversight Act, FISA, FISA Amendments Act (702), RISAA, IRTPA, Section 394 (cyber), Cyber Info Sharing Act.

Code titles. Title 50. Source: CO-06 House office, appointment to the Intelligence Committee (Jan. 22, 2025).

Watch list for these committees

Statutes in the committee lists above that have lapsed, partly expired, or been repealed. These are the places where a deadline or a gap in law creates work for the committees.

Statute Status Where it stands
Defense Production Act Partly expired Most authorities carry a sunset. They were extended from September 30, 2025 to January 30, 2026, then to September 30, 2026 by the FY2026 defense authorization. Whether they were extended again in fall 2026 was not confirmed at this wiki’s last review. Section 721 (foreign investment review) is permanent. Check current status.
FISA Partly expired The core titles on electronic surveillance, physical search, and pen registers are in force. Title VII, including Section 702, lapsed in June 2026 (see the FISA Amendments Act page). The business records, roving wiretap, and lone wolf provisions lapsed in March 2020.
FISA Amendments Act (702) Lapsed Title VII, including Section 702, lapsed in mid-June 2026 for the first time since 2008, after two short extensions in April 2026 and a failed Senate vote. Collection can continue under existing court certifications, reported to run to March 2027. No reauthorization had been enacted as of this wiki’s last review (November 10, 2026). Check current status before relying on this page.
Cyber Info Sharing Act Partly expired Running on short extensions. The original ten-year term ended September 30, 2025 and the law lapsed twice before being extended through September 30, 2026. A continuing resolution then carried it into December 2026. A long-term renewal is tied to the FY2027 defense bill. Check current status.
RISAA Lapsed Its two-year reauthorization of Section 702 ran to April 20, 2026. Congress passed a 10-day and then a 45-day extension, and the authority lapsed in mid-June 2026. Check current status before relying on this page.

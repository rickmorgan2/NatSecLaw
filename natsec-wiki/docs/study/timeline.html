Timeline

The statutes in the order they were enacted, grouped by era.

Each generation of national security law answered the problems of the one before. Read an era’s laws together to see the pattern: a crisis, a grant of power, and later a constraint. Dates are years of enactment; for code-title and composite entries they are the year the title or authority began.

Founding and the early republic

1788
U.S. Constitution: War and Foreign Affairs Powers In force Constitution
The Constitution divides national security powers between Congress and the President and leaves the boundary largely undefined.
1792
Militia Acts of 1792 Superseded Framework statute
Two acts passed a week apart. The first let the President call out state militias to repel invasion, suppress insurrection, and enforce federal law.
1794
Neutrality Act of 1794 In force Framework statute
Makes it a crime for private persons to wage war from U.S. territory against a country with which the United States is at peace.
1798
Alien Enemies Act of 1798 In force Framework statute
The only one of the Alien and Sedition Acts still in force. During a declared war, or an invasion or predatory incursion by a foreign nation or government, the President may detain, restrict, and remove nationals of the hostile country.
1799
Logan Act In force Targeted provision
Bars private citizens from corresponding with a foreign government, without authority, in order to influence its conduct in a dispute with the United States or to defeat U.S. measures.
1807
Insurrection Act In force Framework statute
A set of provisions, first enacted in 1807 and broadened in 1861 and 1871, that lets the President use the armed forces and federalized militia inside the United States to suppress insurrection, enforce federal law, or protect civil rights when ordinary enforcement fails.
1812
Declarations of War (1812-1942) In force Specific authorization
Congress has declared war eleven times, in five conflicts: the War of 1812, the Mexican-American War, the Spanish-American War, and the two world wars.

Civil War to the First World War

1878
Posse Comitatus Act In force Framework statute
A one-sentence criminal statute that bars using federal military forces to execute the laws unless the Constitution or an act of Congress expressly authorizes it.
1903
Militia Act of 1903 (Dick Act) Superseded Framework statute
Replaced the 1792 militia system. It divided the militia into an organized part, the National Guard, and an unorganized reserve, and gave the Guard federal funding and equipment in exchange for meeting Army standards.
1916
National Defense Act of 1916 Superseded Framework statute
The first comprehensive statute organizing U.S. land forces. It defined the Army as the Regular Army, the National Guard, and a federal reserve, and it let the President draft the Guard into federal service for use overseas.
1917
Espionage Act of 1917 In force Framework statute
The principal criminal statute protecting national defense information. It covers classic spying for a foreign power and also the unauthorized retention or disclosure of defense information by people who had lawful access to it.
1917
Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917 In force Framework statute
A First World War statute giving the President sweeping control over trade and property involving enemy countries and nationals.

Interwar years and the Second World War

1926
Title 22, U.S. Code: Foreign Relations and Intercourse In force Code title overview
The title of the U.S. Code for diplomacy and foreign assistance. It holds the State Department’s authorities, the Foreign Service, foreign aid, arms export controls, and most country-specific sanctions and policy statutes.
1926
Title 50, U.S. Code: War and National Defense In force Code title overview
The title of the U.S. Code that collects the laws on war powers, emergency powers, intelligence, and economic defense.
1938
Foreign Agents Registration Act of 1938 In force Framework statute
A disclosure law. People who act in the United States on behalf of foreign governments, parties, or other foreign principals in political or public relations work must register with the Justice Department and report their activities and payments.
1941
Lend-Lease Act Superseded Specific authorization
Authorized the President to sell, transfer, lend, or lease defense articles to any country whose defense he judged vital to the defense of the United States.
1942
Foreign Claims Act In force Targeted provision
Lets the military departments appoint claims commissions to settle claims by inhabitants of foreign countries for property loss, injury, or death caused by U.S. forces or incident to their noncombat activities abroad.
1945
United Nations Participation Act of 1945 In force Framework statute
Governs U.S. participation in the United Nations: how representatives are appointed, how the United States carries out Security Council decisions, and the terms on which U.S. forces may be made available to the Council.

Building the national security state

1947
National Security Act of 1947 In force Framework statute
The founding charter of the modern national security system. It created the National Security Council and the Central Intelligence Agency, established a separate Air Force, and placed the military departments under a single Secretary of Defense.
1948
Military Selective Service Act Partly expired Framework statute
The standing law of conscription. It requires men to register at 18 and maintains the Selective Service System so that a draft could be run if Congress and the President restored the power to induct.
1948
U.S. Information and Educational Exchange Act of 1948 (Smith-Mundt) In force Framework statute
The legal foundation of U.S. public diplomacy. It authorized the State Department to explain the United States to foreign audiences through broadcasting, publications, and educational and cultural exchanges.
1949
Central Intelligence Agency Act of 1949 In force Framework statute
Gave the CIA the administrative tools to operate in secret: authority to spend money without the usual accounting rules, to withhold information about its staff and structure, and to procure and transfer funds confidentially.
1949
National Security Act Amendments of 1949 In force Framework statute
Fixed the weak structure created in 1947. It turned the National Military Establishment into the Department of Defense, an executive department, and reduced the Army, Navy, and Air Force to military departments under the Secretary of Defense.
1950
Defense Production Act of 1950 Partly expired Framework statute
A Korean War statute that lets the President direct private industry in the interest of national defense: by putting government orders first in line, by financing new production capacity, and by organizing industry cooperation.
1950
Uniform Code of Military Justice In force Framework statute
The criminal code and court system of the U.S. armed forces. It replaced the separate Army and Navy disciplinary codes with one law for all services, defining military offenses, the types of courts-martial, the rights of the accused, and civilian appellate review.
1951
Mutual Security Act of 1951 Superseded Specific authorization
Merged the Marshall Plan’s economic aid, military assistance to allies, and technical assistance into one program under one agency, with the stated purpose of strengthening the free world against Soviet power.
1952
Reserve and National Guard Mobilization Authorities In force Framework statute
The set of Title 10 provisions, descended from the Armed Forces Reserve Act of 1952 and earlier militia acts, that let the federal government order reserve forces and the National Guard to active duty.
1954
Atomic Energy Act of 1954 In force Framework statute
The governing law for both civilian and military uses of nuclear material in the United States.
1956
State Department Basic Authorities Act of 1956 In force Framework statute
The organic statute of the State Department. It sets out the Secretary’s management authorities, the department’s senior positions, and a long list of operating powers that have been added over the decades.
1956
Title 10, U.S. Code: Armed Forces In force Code title overview
The title of the U.S. Code that governs the armed forces: their organization, command, personnel, training, procurement, and reserve components.
1956
Title 32, U.S. Code: National Guard In force Code title overview
The title of the U.S. Code for the National Guard when it is not in federal service. It governs organization, federal funding, and training of the Guard while it remains under the command of state governors.
1958
Department of Defense Reorganization Act of 1958 In force Framework statute
President Eisenhower’s reorganization. It took the military departments out of the operational chain of command, so that orders run from the President and the Secretary of Defense to unified commands, and it confirmed the Secretary’s authority, direction, and control over the whole department.
1961
Foreign Assistance Act of 1961 In force Framework statute
The basic law of U.S. foreign aid. It separated economic from military assistance, set the purposes and conditions of each, and led President Kennedy to create the U.S. Agency for International Development by executive order two months later.
1961
National Defense Authorization Act (annual series) In force Specific authorization
The annual law that authorizes funding levels and sets policy for the Defense Department and the nuclear weapons programs of the Energy Department.
1962
Trade Expansion Act of 1962, Section 232 In force Targeted provision
Allows the President to restrict imports that threaten to impair national security. The Commerce Department investigates and reports, and the President then decides whether to impose tariffs, quotas, or negotiated limits.

Vietnam, Watergate, and reassertion

1964
Gulf of Tonkin Resolution Repealed Specific authorization
A joint resolution approving ‘all necessary measures’ by the President to repel attacks on U.S. forces and prevent further aggression in Southeast Asia.
1972
Case-Zablocki Act In force Targeted provision
Requires the Secretary of State to send Congress the text of international agreements other than treaties.
1973
War Powers Resolution In force Framework statute
Passed over President Nixon’s veto, the War Powers Resolution is Congress’s framework statute for sharing the decision to commit U.S. forces to hostilities.
1974
Hughes-Ryan Amendment Superseded Targeted provision
Section 32 of the Foreign Assistance Act of 1974, which added section 662 to the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961.
1976
Arms Export Control Act In force Framework statute
Governs U.S. arms exports. It sets the purposes for which American weapons may be sold, requires licenses for commercial sales, and gives Congress advance notice of major sales and a procedure to block them.
1976
National Emergencies Act In force Framework statute
Ended the open-ended emergencies that had accumulated since the 1930s and set procedures for declaring, renewing, and terminating national emergencies.
1976
Symington and Glenn Amendments In force Targeted provision
Two nonproliferation amendments from 1976 and 1977 that cut off U.S. economic and military aid to countries that trade in nuclear enrichment or reprocessing technology outside international safeguards, or that test a nuclear device.
1977
International Emergency Economic Powers Act In force Framework statute
The workhorse of U.S. economic sanctions. After declaring a national emergency over an unusual and extraordinary threat that originates largely abroad, the President may block property and prohibit transactions involving foreign countries, entities, and individuals.
1978
Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act of 1978 Partly expired Framework statute
Created a judicial process for foreign intelligence surveillance inside the United States.
1979
Taiwan Relations Act In force Specific authorization
Passed after the United States recognized the People’s Republic of China and ended formal relations with Taipei.
1980
Classified Information Procedures Act In force Framework statute
Sets pretrial and trial procedures for handling classified information in federal criminal cases, so that a defendant cannot force the government to choose between dropping the case and disclosing secrets.
1980
Foreign Service Act of 1980 In force Framework statute
The personnel law for U.S. diplomats. It establishes a career Foreign Service based on merit, with rank held by the person and not the job, competitive promotion, and worldwide availability.
1980
Intelligence Oversight Act of 1980 In force Framework statute
Enacted as part of the FY1981 intelligence authorization, it added the oversight title to the National Security Act.

End of the Cold War

1982
Emergency Military Construction Authority (10 U.S.C. 2808) In force Targeted provision
One of the standby powers unlocked by a national emergency. When a declared war or emergency requires use of the armed forces, the Secretary of Defense may build military construction projects that Congress never authorized, using unspent military construction funds.
1986
Goldwater-Nichols Department of Defense Reorganization Act of 1986 In force Framework statute
The most important reorganization of the Defense Department since 1947. It took operational control away from the service chiefs, ran the chain of command from the President through the Secretary of Defense to the combatant commanders, and made the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs the principal military adviser.
1986
Nunn-Cohen Amendment In force Framework statute
Section 1311 of the FY1987 defense authorization. It created U.S. Special Operations Command as a unified combatant command with its own budget line and a civilian assistant secretary to oversee it.
1990
Defense Support for Counterdrug Activities (10 U.S.C. 284) In force Targeted provision
Authorizes the Defense Department to support the counterdrug and counter transnational organized crime work of other federal agencies and of state, local, and foreign law enforcement, when they ask for it.
1991
Authorization for Use of Military Force Against Iraq Resolution (1991) Repealed Specific authorization
Authorized the President to use force under U.N. Security Council Resolution 678 to expel Iraqi forces from Kuwait, after a presidential determination that diplomatic means had been exhausted.
1991
Soviet Nuclear Threat Reduction Act of 1991 (Nunn-Lugar) In force Specific authorization
Authorized the Defense Department to spend its own funds helping the former Soviet republics secure, transport, and dismantle nuclear, chemical, and other weapons after the Soviet collapse.
1996
Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 In force Specific authorization
Passed after the Oklahoma City bombing. Its national security significance lies in two linked provisions: the Secretary of State’s power to designate foreign terrorist organizations, and the crime of giving such organizations material support.
1996
Cuban Liberty and Democratic Solidarity (LIBERTAD) Act of 1996 In force Specific authorization
Wrote the Cuba embargo into statute so that a President cannot lift it alone, and extended its reach to foreign companies that use property confiscated by the Cuban government.
1996
Iran Sanctions Act of 1996 In force Specific authorization
Originally the Iran and Libya Sanctions Act. It pioneered secondary sanctions by penalizing foreign companies, not just U.S. ones, that invest in Iran’s energy sector.
1997
Leahy Laws In force Targeted provision
Two parallel provisions that bar U.S. assistance to any unit of a foreign security force when there is credible information that the unit committed a gross violation of human rights.
1998
Foreign Affairs Reform and Restructuring Act of 1998 In force Framework statute
Division G of the FY1999 omnibus appropriations act. It folded the Arms Control and Disarmament Agency and the U.S. Information Agency into the State Department and, for the first time, gave USAID a statutory existence as an independent establishment.

After September 11

2001
Authorization for Use of Military Force (2001) In force Specific authorization
Enacted a week after the September 11 attacks, this joint resolution authorizes the President to use all necessary and appropriate force against the nations, organizations, or persons that planned, authorized, committed, or aided the attacks, or that harbored those who did.
2001
USA PATRIOT Act Partly expired Specific authorization
An omnibus counterterrorism law passed six weeks after September 11. It widened surveillance and information-sharing authorities, expanded anti-money-laundering rules, and broadened terrorism offenses and immigration exclusions.
2002
Authorization for Use of Military Force Against Iraq Resolution of 2002 Repealed Specific authorization
Authorized the President to use the armed forces to defend U.S. national security against the continuing threat posed by Iraq and to enforce relevant U.N. Security Council resolutions.
2002
Homeland Security Act of 2002 In force Framework statute
Created the Department of Homeland Security by merging 22 agencies, the largest federal reorganization since 1947.
2004
Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act of 2004 In force Framework statute
Carried out the main recommendations of the 9/11 Commission. It separated leadership of the intelligence community from leadership of the CIA by creating the Director of National Intelligence.
2004
Millennium Challenge Act of 2003 In force Specific authorization
Created the Millennium Challenge Corporation, a small government corporation that gives large, multi-year grants to poor countries that pass a scorecard of governance, economic freedom, and social investment indicators.
2004
Support of Special Operations to Combat Terrorism (10 U.S.C. 127e) In force Targeted provision
Lets the Secretary of Defense fund foreign forces, irregular forces, groups, or individuals who support or facilitate ongoing U.S. special operations against terrorism.
2005
Detainee Treatment Act of 2005 In force Targeted provision
Known as the McCain Amendment, it prohibits cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment of anyone in U.S. custody, wherever held, and ties Defense Department interrogations to the Army Field Manual.
2006
Military Commissions Act of 2006 In force Specific authorization
Passed after Hamdan v. Rumsfeld struck down the commissions created by presidential order, it gave statutory authority to try alien unlawful enemy combatants by military commission and defined the offenses and procedures.
2008
FISA Amendments Act of 2008 Lapsed Specific authorization
Added Title VII to FISA. Section 702 lets the government target non-U.S. persons reasonably believed to be abroad, with compelled help from U.S. communications providers, under annual court-approved certifications instead of individual warrants.

Sanctions, cyber, and great powers

2015
Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 Partly expired Specific authorization
Enacted as Division N of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2016. It set up a voluntary system for companies and the federal government to share cyber threat indicators and defensive measures, with legal protection for companies that take part.
2015
Iran Nuclear Agreement Review Act of 2015 In force Specific authorization
Gives Congress a defined period to review any nuclear agreement with Iran before the President may waive or suspend statutory sanctions, and requires regular compliance certifications afterward.
2015
USA FREEDOM Act of 2015 Partly expired Specific authorization
The legislative response to the 2013 Snowden disclosures. It ended bulk collection of domestic telephone records and required that records requests be tied to a specific selection term.
2016
Global Magnitsky Human Rights Accountability Act In force Specific authorization
Authorizes visa bans and asset freezes against foreign individuals and entities responsible for serious human rights abuse or significant corruption anywhere in the world.
2016
Security Cooperation Reform (FY2017 NDAA, Title 10 Chapter 16) In force Framework statute
The FY2017 defense authorization gathered dozens of scattered Defense Department authorities for training, equipping, and advising foreign forces into a single chapter of Title 10, with common definitions, oversight, and evaluation rules.
2017
Annual Report on Civilian Casualties (FY2018 NDAA Sec. 1057) In force Targeted provision
Requires the Secretary of Defense to report to the congressional defense committees by May 1 each year on civilian casualties caused by U.S. military operations in the previous year.
2017
Countering America’s Adversaries Through Sanctions Act In force Specific authorization
A three-part sanctions law aimed at Iran, Russia, and North Korea. Its Russia title wrote existing executive order sanctions into statute and gave Congress the power to block the President from easing them.
2017
Women, Peace, and Security Act of 2017 In force Specific authorization
Makes it U.S. policy to promote the meaningful participation of women in conflict prevention, peace negotiations, and post-conflict recovery, and requires a government-wide strategy to carry that out.
2018
Civilian Casualty Policy (FY2019 NDAA Sec. 936) In force Targeted provision
Directs the Under Secretary of Defense for Policy to designate a senior civilian official, at or above the Assistant Secretary level, to develop and oversee compliance with Defense Department policy on civilian casualties from U.S. military operations.
2018
Export Control Reform Act of 2018 In force Framework statute
The permanent statutory basis for Commerce Department controls on dual-use exports. It replaced the Export Administration Act of 1979, which had lapsed and been kept alive for years by emergency declarations.
2018
Foreign Investment Risk Review Modernization Act of 2018 In force Framework statute
Expanded the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States, which reviews foreign acquisitions for national security risk.
2018
Military Cyber Operations Authority (10 U.S.C. 394-395) In force Targeted provision
Affirms the Defense Department’s authority to conduct military operations in cyberspace, including clandestine ones and ones short of hostilities, and classifies clandestine military cyber activity as traditional military activity instead of covert action.
2019
Caesar Syria Civilian Protection Act of 2019 Repealed Specific authorization
Imposed secondary sanctions on foreign persons doing business with the Assad government’s military, energy, aviation, and reconstruction sectors.
2019
Elie Wiesel Genocide and Atrocities Prevention Act of 2018 In force Specific authorization
Declares that preventing genocide and other atrocities is a national interest of the United States and requires the executive branch to organize, train, and report accordingly.
2019
Ex Gratia Payments for Civilian Harm (FY2020 NDAA Sec. 1213) In force Targeted provision
Authorizes the Secretary of Defense to make ex gratia payments to foreign civilians for property damage, injury, or death incident to the use of force by U.S. forces, a coalition that includes the United States, or a military organization supporting either.
2019
Global Fragility Act of 2019 Partly expired Specific authorization
Enacted as Title V of Division J of the Further Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2020. It directs a ten-year, whole-of-government strategy to prevent violent conflict and stabilize fragile states, applied first in a small set of priority countries.
2022
Civilian Protection Center of Excellence (10 U.S.C. 184) In force Targeted provision
Requires the Secretary of Defense to operate a Civilian Protection Center of Excellence as the Department’s focal point for civilian casualties and other civilian harm from U.S. military operations.
2024
Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act Lapsed Specific authorization
Reauthorized Section 702 for two years, the shortest extension to date, and made the most extensive changes to FISA since 2008, concentrated on how the FBI may search collected data for information about Americans.

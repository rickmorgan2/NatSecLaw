Unit 10: Arms transfers, export controls, and investment screening

How does the United States control what weapons and technology leave the country, and who buys in?

Previous: Unit 9. Foreign assistance, fragility, and partners | All units | Next: Unit 11. Civilian harm and the conduct of operations

The Arms Export Control Act governs weapons sales and gives Congress notice of major sales and a way to block them. Export controls on dual-use technology now rest on the Export Control Reform Act, and foreign investment review runs through CFIUS as expanded by FIRRMA. Nonproliferation conditions and the Taiwan Relations Act show how Congress attaches policy to transfers.

What you should be able to do

  • Explain the congressional review process for major arms sales.
  • Distinguish arms export controls from dual-use export controls.
  • Describe what FIRRMA added to foreign investment review.

Reading path

Read these in order. Each card links to the wiki page and to the official text. Ticking a box saves your progress in this browser only.

Arms Export Control Act 1976 In force Framework statute

Governs U.S. arms exports. It sets the purposes for which American weapons may be sold, requires licenses for commercial sales, and gives Congress advance notice of major sales and a procedure to block them.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Symington-Glenn Amendments 1976 In force Targeted provision

Two nonproliferation amendments from 1976 and 1977 that cut off U.S. economic and military aid to countries that trade in nuclear enrichment or reprocessing technology outside international safeguards, or that test a nuclear device.

Read it: current code

Export Control Reform Act 2018 In force Framework statute

The permanent statutory basis for Commerce Department controls on dual-use exports. It replaced the Export Administration Act of 1979, which had lapsed and been kept alive for years by emergency declarations.

Read it: enacted text, current code

FIRRMA (CFIUS) 2018 In force Framework statute

Expanded the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States, which reviews foreign acquisitions for national security risk.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Atomic Energy Act 1954 In force Framework statute

The governing law for both civilian and military uses of nuclear material in the United States.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Taiwan Relations Act 1979 In force Specific authorization

Passed after the United States recognized the People’s Republic of China and ended formal relations with Taipei.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Further reading

  • Leahy Laws: Two parallel provisions that bar U.S. assistance to any unit of a foreign security force when there is credible information that the unit committed a gross violation of human rights.
  • Defense Production Act: A Korean War statute that lets the President direct private industry in the interest of national defense: by putting government orders first in line, by financing new production capacity, and by organizing industry cooperation.
  • Nunn-Lugar Act: Authorized the Defense Department to spend its own funds helping the former Soviet republics secure, transport, and dismantle nuclear, chemical, and other weapons after the Soviet collapse.
  • Section 232 (trade): Allows the President to restrict imports that threaten to impair national security. The Commerce Department investigates and reports, and the President then decides whether to impose tariffs, quotas, or negotiated limits.

Key terms

Congressional notification
Advance notice to Congress of a proposed major arms sale, which opens a period in which Congress may pass a joint resolution of disapproval.
Dual-use item
Goods, software, or technology with both civilian and military uses, controlled by the Commerce Department under ECRA.
CFIUS
The Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States, which reviews foreign acquisitions and investments for national security risk.

Check yourself

Try to answer each question before opening it.

Tip1. What role does the AECA give Congress in major arms sales?

Advance notice and a procedure to block a sale by joint resolution. The act also sets the purposes for which U.S. weapons may be sold and requires licenses for commercial sales. See Arms Export Control Act.

Tip2. What statute did ECRA replace, and why?

The Export Administration Act of 1979, which had lapsed and was kept alive for years by emergency declarations. See Export Control Reform Act and IEEPA.

Tip3. What triggers the Symington and Glenn Amendments?

Trading in nuclear enrichment or reprocessing technology outside international safeguards, or testing a nuclear device, which cuts off U.S. economic and military aid. See Symington-Glenn Amendments.

Tip4. Beyond takeovers, what did FIRRMA bring under review?

Minority stakes in sensitive businesses and some real estate transactions. See FIRRMA (CFIUS).

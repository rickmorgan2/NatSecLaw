Unit 3: Deciding to use force

Who decides when the United States goes to war, and how is that decision written into law?

Previous: Unit 2. Building the national security state, 1947 to 1958 | All units | Next: Unit 4. Emergency and economic powers

After Vietnam, Congress tried to rebuild its share of the decision to fight. The War Powers Resolution set a framework of consultation, reports, and a 60-day clock. Every later authorization for use of military force was written to satisfy it. This unit traces the line from the Tonkin Gulf Resolution to the 2001 AUMF, and to the repeal of the two Iraq authorizations in 2025.

What you should be able to do

  • Explain the War Powers Resolution’s reporting requirement and 60-day clock.
  • Compare the scope of the 1991, 2001, and 2002 authorizations.
  • Describe how U.N. Security Council mandates relate to congressional authorization.

Reading path

Read these in order. Each card links to the wiki page and to the official text. Ticking a box saves your progress in this browser only.

War Powers Resolution 1973 In force Framework statute

Passed over President Nixon’s veto, the War Powers Resolution is Congress’s framework statute for sharing the decision to commit U.S. forces to hostilities.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Gulf of Tonkin Resolution 1964 Repealed Specific authorization

A joint resolution approving ‘all necessary measures’ by the President to repel attacks on U.S. forces and prevent further aggression in Southeast Asia.

Read it: enacted text

1991 Gulf War AUMF 1991 Repealed Specific authorization

Authorized the President to use force under U.N. Security Council Resolution 678 to expel Iraqi forces from Kuwait, after a presidential determination that diplomatic means had been exhausted.

Read it: enacted text, current code

2001 AUMF 2001 In force Specific authorization

Enacted a week after the September 11 attacks, this joint resolution authorizes the President to use all necessary and appropriate force against the nations, organizations, or persons that planned, authorized, committed, or aided the attacks, or that harbored those who did.

Read it: enacted text, current code

2002 Iraq AUMF 2002 Repealed Specific authorization

Authorized the President to use the armed forces to defend U.S. national security against the continuing threat posed by Iraq and to enforce relevant U.N. Security Council resolutions.

Read it: enacted text, current code

UN Participation Act 1945 In force Framework statute

Governs U.S. participation in the United Nations: how representatives are appointed, how the United States carries out Security Council decisions, and the terms on which U.S. forces may be made available to the Council.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Further reading

  • Case-Zablocki Act: Requires the Secretary of State to send Congress the text of international agreements other than treaties.
  • Declarations of War: Congress has declared war eleven times, in five conflicts: the War of 1812, the Mexican-American War, the Spanish-American War, and the two world wars.
  • Constitution (war powers): The Constitution divides national security powers between Congress and the President and leaves the boundary largely undefined.

Key terms

AUMF
An authorization for use of military force, a joint resolution that gives the President statutory authority to use force for a stated purpose. Modern AUMFs declare themselves the specific authorization the War Powers Resolution requires.
Hostilities
The War Powers Resolution’s trigger term, which it never defines. Most disputes over the resolution turn on whether an operation amounts to hostilities.
60-day clock
Under WPR Sec. 5(b), forces must be withdrawn within 60 days of a required report, plus 30 days for safe withdrawal, unless Congress authorizes the operation.

Check yourself

Try to answer each question before opening it.

Tip1. What starts the War Powers Resolution’s 60-day clock?

A report under Sec. 4(a)(1), due within 48 hours of introducing forces into hostilities or imminent hostilities. Presidents often file reports ‘consistent with’ the resolution instead, which is why the clock rarely binds. See War Powers Resolution.

Tip2. Whom does the 2001 AUMF authorize force against?

The nations, organizations, or persons the President determines planned, authorized, committed, or aided the September 11 attacks, or harbored those who did. It has no sunset. See 2001 AUMF.

Tip3. What happened to the 1991 and 2002 Iraq authorizations?

Both were repealed by the FY2026 defense authorization in December 2025. See 1991 Gulf War AUMF and 2002 Iraq AUMF.

Tip4. Why was the Tonkin Gulf Resolution’s history central to the War Powers Resolution?

Its open-ended approval of ‘all necessary measures’ became the legal basis for escalating the Vietnam War, and Congress designed the War Powers Resolution to prevent another open-ended grant. See Gulf of Tonkin Resolution.

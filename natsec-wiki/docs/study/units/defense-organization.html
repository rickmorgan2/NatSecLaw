Unit 7: Organizing and commanding the military

Who commands U.S. forces, and how does Congress shape the military each year?

Previous: Unit 6. Surveillance law | All units | Next: Unit 8. The military at home

Goldwater-Nichols set today’s command structure and made jointness the rule. Nunn-Cohen gave special operations its own command and budget line. The annual defense authorization carries most new defense law, including targeted Title 10 authorities for special operations surrogates, cyber operations, and emergency construction. Read this unit with the Title 10 and Title 50 page.

What you should be able to do

  • Describe the chain of command under Goldwater-Nichols.
  • Explain why the annual NDAA is the main vehicle for national security legislation.
  • Distinguish Section 127e support from partner capacity building under Chapter 16.

Reading path

Read these in order. Each card links to the wiki page and to the official text. Ticking a box saves your progress in this browser only.

Goldwater-Nichols Act 1986 In force Framework statute

The most important reorganization of the Defense Department since 1947. It took operational control away from the service chiefs, ran the chain of command from the President through the Secretary of Defense to the combatant commanders, and made the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs the principal military adviser.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Nunn-Cohen Amendment 1986 In force Framework statute

Section 1311 of the FY1987 defense authorization. It created U.S. Special Operations Command as a unified combatant command with its own budget line and a civilian assistant secretary to oversee it.

Read it: enacted text, current code

NDAA (annual) 1961 In force Specific authorization

The annual law that authorizes funding levels and sets policy for the Defense Department and the nuclear weapons programs of the Energy Department.

Read it:

Section 127e 2004 In force Targeted provision

Lets the Secretary of Defense fund foreign forces, irregular forces, groups, or individuals who support or facilitate ongoing U.S. special operations against terrorism.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Section 394 (cyber) 2018 In force Targeted provision

Affirms the Defense Department’s authority to conduct military operations in cyberspace, including clandestine ones and ones short of hostilities, and classifies clandestine military cyber activity as traditional military activity instead of covert action.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Section 2808 (construction) 1982 In force Targeted provision

One of the standby powers unlocked by a national emergency. When a declared war or emergency requires use of the armed forces, the Secretary of Defense may build military construction projects that Congress never authorized, using unspent military construction funds.

Read it: enacted text, current code

Further reading

  • DoD Reorganization Act (1958): President Eisenhower’s reorganization. It took the military departments out of the operational chain of command, so that orders run from the President and the Secretary of Defense to unified commands, and it confirmed the Secretary’s authority, direction, and control over the whole department.
  • Security Cooperation (Ch. 16): The FY2017 defense authorization gathered dozens of scattered Defense Department authorities for training, equipping, and advising foreign forces into a single chapter of Title 10, with common definitions, oversight, and evaluation rules.
  • Section 284 (counterdrug): Authorizes the Defense Department to support the counterdrug and counter transnational organized crime work of other federal agencies and of state, local, and foreign law enforcement, when they ask for it.
  • Title 10 U.S.C.: The title of the U.S. Code that governs the armed forces: their organization, command, personnel, training, procurement, and reserve components.

Key terms

Combatant commander
The commander of a unified combatant command, who receives operational orders from the President through the Secretary of Defense.
Surrogate force
A foreign force supporting U.S. special operations toward U.S. objectives, funded under 10 U.S.C. 127e, as distinct from a partner force built up for its own missions.
Traditional military activity
A military operation outside the covert action definition. Section 394 classifies clandestine military cyber activity this way.

Check yourself

Try to answer each question before opening it.

Tip1. Under Goldwater-Nichols, who is the principal military adviser to the President?

The Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. The act also ran the chain of command from the President through the Secretary of Defense to the combatant commanders. See Goldwater-Nichols Act.

Tip2. What did the Nunn-Cohen Amendment create?

U.S. Special Operations Command, with its own budget line and a civilian assistant secretary for oversight. See Nunn-Cohen Amendment.

Tip3. What does Section 2808 allow during a declared national emergency?

If the emergency requires use of the armed forces, the Secretary of Defense may undertake military construction Congress never authorized, using unspent military construction funds. See Section 2808 (construction).

Tip4. Why does so much national security law ride on the NDAA?

It has been enacted every year for more than six decades, so it is the most reliable vehicle for legislation of all kinds. See NDAA (annual).

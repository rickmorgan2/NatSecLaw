Glossary

Every key term from the study units, in alphabetical order.

60-day clock
Under WPR Sec. 5(b), forces must be withdrawn within 60 days of a required report, plus 30 days for safe withdrawal, unless Congress authorizes the operation. From Unit 3: Deciding to use force.
AUMF
An authorization for use of military force, a joint resolution that gives the President statutory authority to use force for a stated purpose. Modern AUMFs declare themselves the specific authorization the War Powers Resolution requires. From Unit 3: Deciding to use force.
Blocking
Freezing property and interests in property within U.S. jurisdiction and prohibiting transactions in them, the core tool of IEEPA sanctions. From Unit 4: Emergency and economic powers.
Center of Excellence
The Civilian Protection Center of Excellence at 10 U.S.C. 184, the Department’s statutory focal point for civilian harm data, guidance, and lessons learned. From Unit 11: Civilian harm and the conduct of operations.
CFIUS
The Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States, which reviews foreign acquisitions and investments for national security risk. From Unit 10: Arms transfers, export controls, and investment screening.
Civilian harm mitigation and response
The Defense Department’s term, abbreviated CHMR, for preventing, mitigating, and responding to civilian casualties and other harm from military operations. From Unit 11: Civilian harm and the conduct of operations.
Combat exclusion
The Foreign Claims Act’s bar on claims arising from combat, which is why combat harm needs a separate ex gratia authority. From Unit 11: Civilian harm and the conduct of operations.
Combatant commander
The commander of a unified combatant command, who receives operational orders from the President through the Secretary of Defense. From Unit 7: Organizing and commanding the military.
Congressional notification
Advance notice to Congress of a proposed major arms sale, which opens a period in which Congress may pass a joint resolution of disapproval. From Unit 10: Arms transfers, export controls, and investment screening.
Covert action
Activity to influence conditions abroad where the U.S. role is intended not to be apparent or acknowledged. It requires a written presidential finding and notice to the intelligence committees. Defined at 50 U.S.C. 3093(e). From Unit 5: Intelligence and its oversight.
Declaration of war
A joint resolution or act under Article I declaring that a state of war exists. Congress has passed eleven, in five wars, the last in 1942. From Unit 1: Where the power comes from.
Dual-use item
Goods, software, or technology with both civilian and military uses, controlled by the Commerce Department under ECRA. From Unit 10: Arms transfers, export controls, and investment screening.
Ex gratia payment
A voluntary payment to a harmed civilian or family that does not admit legal liability. Since 2019, Sec. 1213 of the FY2020 NDAA is the sole authority for such payments for harm from the use of force. From Unit 11: Civilian harm and the conduct of operations.
Federalization
Ordering Guard members into federal service under Title 10, which places them under the President and within Posse Comitatus limits unless an exception applies. From Unit 8: The military at home.
Finding
A written presidential determination that a covert action is necessary to support identifiable foreign policy objectives. From Unit 5: Intelligence and its oversight.
Framework statute
A standing law that creates an institution or a general procedure that later laws and executive actions depend on. From Unit 2: Building the national security state, 1947 to 1958.
Hostilities
The War Powers Resolution’s trigger term, which it never defines. Most disputes over the resolution turn on whether an operation amounts to hostilities. From Unit 3: Deciding to use force.
Leahy vetting
Screening foreign security force units for credible information of gross violations of human rights before they receive U.S. assistance. From Unit 9: Foreign assistance, fragility, and partners.
National emergency
A presidential declaration under the National Emergencies Act that unlocks standby statutory powers. The President must specify which powers are being used, and emergencies lapse unless renewed each year. From Unit 4: Emergency and economic powers.
Operational chain of command
The line of authority for military operations, which since 1958 runs from the President through the Secretary of Defense to the unified commands, not through the service departments. From Unit 2: Building the national security state, 1947 to 1958.
Positive law title
A U.S. Code title Congress has itself enacted, so the Code text is the law. Titles 10 and 32 are positive law; Titles 22 and 50 are not, so for them the Statutes at Large control. From Unit 1: Where the power comes from.
Secondary sanctions
Penalties on foreign persons, not just U.S. persons, for dealing with a sanctioned country or sector. The Iran Sanctions Act pioneered them. From Unit 4: Emergency and economic powers.
Section 702
The FISA authority, added in 2008, to target non-U.S. persons reasonably believed to be abroad with compelled help from U.S. providers, under annual court-approved certifications instead of individual orders. From Unit 6: Surveillance law.
Security assistance
Programs under Title 22, run by the State Department, that provide defense articles, training, and services to foreign governments. From Unit 9: Foreign assistance, fragility, and partners.
Security cooperation
Defense Department activities with foreign security forces, such as training, equipping, and advising, mostly under Title 10 Chapter 16. From Unit 9: Foreign assistance, fragility, and partners.
Statutes at Large
The official chronological publication of every law as enacted, cited by volume and page, for example 87 Stat. 555. From Unit 1: Where the power comes from.
Sunset
A date on which an authority expires unless Congress extends it. From Unit 6: Surveillance law.
Surrogate force
A foreign force supporting U.S. special operations toward U.S. objectives, funded under 10 U.S.C. 127e, as distinct from a partner force built up for its own missions. From Unit 7: Organizing and commanding the military.
Title 32 status
National Guard duty under the command of a state governor, often with federal funding. Posse Comitatus does not apply. From Unit 8: The military at home.
Traditional military activities
The exception that keeps military operations out of the covert action definition, and with it outside the finding process. From Unit 5: Intelligence and its oversight.
Traditional military activity
A military operation outside the covert action definition. Section 394 classifies clandestine military cyber activity this way. From Unit 7: Organizing and commanding the military.
U.S. person query
A search of lawfully collected data using a term associated with an American. RISAA’s changes concentrated on how the FBI may run them. From Unit 6: Surveillance law.
Unified combatant command
A command with forces from more than one service under a single commander, organized by region or function. From Unit 2: Building the national security state, 1947 to 1958.

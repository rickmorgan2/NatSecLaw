Rep. Sara Jacobs (CA-51)

Member lens: committees, record, and district interests mapped onto the statutes.

California’s 51st Congressional District. Democrat.

Before Congress she worked on conflict prevention at the UN Department of Peacekeeping Operations, the UNICEF Innovation Unit, and the State Department’s Bureau of Conflict and Stabilization Operations, and advised Hillary Clinton’s 2016 campaign on foreign policy. She represents much of the city of San Diego together with El Cajon, La Mesa, and Lemon Grove. Serving her third term in the 119th Congress, she was reelected on November 3, 2026, on the AP’s call. She sits on the House Armed Services and Foreign Affairs Committees, is Ranking Member of the Foreign Affairs Subcommittee on Africa, and co-founded and co-chairs the Protection of Civilians in Conflict Caucus. Committee assignments for the 120th Congress had not been announced when this page was last reviewed.

Committee jurisdiction

Each committee seat maps to a slice of the wiki. The lists below are the statutes most central to that committee’s work.

House Foreign Affairs Committee

Role. Member. Ranking Member of the Subcommittee on Africa; member of the Subcommittee on Western Hemisphere.

Jurisdiction. The State Department, foreign assistance, arms sales, sanctions, and the war powers and use-of-force resolutions referred to the House, which in code terms means most of Title 22 and the foreign affairs statutes in Title 50. The Africa subcommittee covers U.S. policy, aid, and security cooperation across sub-Saharan Africa.

Core statutes. Title 22 U.S.C., Foreign Assistance Act, Arms Export Control Act, Foreign Military Financing, IMET, Peacekeeping Operations, Coup restriction (Sec. 7008), State Basic Authorities Act, Foreign Service Act, Leahy Laws, Global Fragility Act, Women, Peace & Security Act, Elie Wiesel Act, Global Magnitsky Act, Millennium Challenge Act, Foreign Affairs Reform Act, War Powers Resolution, 2001 AUMF, IEEPA, Case-Zablocki Act.

Code titles. Title 22 U.S.C., Title 50 U.S.C.. Source: Rep. Sara Jacobs’s office, Committees and Caucuses.

House Armed Services Committee

Role. Member. Subcommittees on Military Personnel and on Intelligence and Special Operations.

Jurisdiction. The Defense Department and the armed forces, which in code terms means Title 10 and the annual defense authorization. The personnel subcommittee covers pay, benefits, family support, and military justice; the intelligence and special operations subcommittee covers special operations forces and defense intelligence.

Core statutes. NDAA (annual), Title 10 U.S.C., UCMJ, Selective Service Act, Section 127e, Security Cooperation (Ch. 16), Leahy Laws, Section 394 (cyber), Civilian Casualty Policy, Civilian Casualty Report, Civilian Protection Center, Ex Gratia Payments.

Code titles. Title 10 U.S.C.. Source: Rep. Sara Jacobs’s office, Committees and Caucuses.

Watch list for these committees

Statutes in the committee lists above that have lapsed, partly expired, or been repealed, or whose status note asks to check current status. These are the places where a deadline, a gap in law, or a compliance dispute creates work for the committees.

Statute Status Where it stands
Selective Service Act Partly expired The registration requirement is in force. Authority to induct expired on July 1, 1973, so a draft would require new legislation. Changes to registration have been proposed in recent defense bills. Check current status.
NDAA (annual) In force The most recent enacted act is for fiscal year 2026 (Pub. L. 119-60, signed December 18, 2025). For fiscal year 2027 the House passed H.R. 8800 on July 22, 2026; the Senate bill (S. 4784) had not passed, and final action was expected in the post-election session. Check current status.
Foreign Military Financing In force In force. Funded each year in Title IV of the State-foreign operations appropriations act. Check current status.
War Powers Resolution In force In force. Every administration since Nixon has questioned the constitutionality of parts of it, and the 60-day clock has rarely been treated as binding. In 2026 House members introduced resolutions under it concerning U.S. military action in Venezuela and against Iran; their outcomes are not recorded here. Check current status.
IMET In force In force. Funded each year in Title IV of the State-foreign operations appropriations act. Check current status.
Peacekeeping Operations In force In force. Funded each year in Title IV of the State-foreign operations appropriations act. Check current status.
Foreign Affairs Reform Act In force In force. In 2025 the administration moved USAID’s remaining functions into the State Department and wound down most of the agency without new legislation; the statutory provisions on the agency have not been repealed and the action has been litigated. Check current status.
Millennium Challenge Act In force In force. The Millennium Challenge Corporation was targeted for deep cuts in 2025. Check current status of the agency and its compacts.
Civilian Casualty Policy In force In force. A June 2026 analysis of a Defense Department Inspector General evaluation reported that civilian harm staff had been cut to about a tenth of their earlier size in 2025 and 2026. It also reported that the Department and the Inspector General disagreed over what the statute requires of the designated senior official. Check current status.
Global Fragility Act Partly expired The policy framework remains in law, but the funding authorizations ran through fiscal year 2024. Reauthorization bills (H.R. 3005 and S. 2678, 119th Congress) were introduced in 2025 and had not been enacted as of this wiki’s last review. Implementation largely stalled in 2025 after USAID and the State Department’s conflict and stabilization bureau were dismantled.
Civilian Protection Center In force In force. The center was established under the statute. A June 2026 analysis of a Defense Department Inspector General evaluation reported that its staff had fallen from nearly 40 to seven and that senior Defense officials had asked to eliminate it, which would require Congress to change the statute. Check current status.
Coup restriction (Sec. 7008) In force Re-enacted each year in the State-foreign operations appropriations act; cited here as enacted for fiscal year 2026, which includes a program-by-program waiver. Check current status.

Not yet covered by the wiki

  • Section 1202 of the FY2018 defense authorization (irregular warfare support), which her Upholding Human Rights Abroad Act would bring under Leahy vetting.
  • Section 620I of the Foreign Assistance Act and other conditions on security assistance and arms transfers to governments that restrict humanitarian aid.
  • Africa-specific statutes, including the African Growth and Opportunity Act, and the authorities behind U.S. Africa Command’s partnerships.
  • DoD Instruction 3000.17 and the 2022 Civilian Harm Mitigation and Response Action Plan, which implement the civilian harm statutes but are not statutes themselves.
  • Military child care and family support provisions in Title 10 and the annual defense authorization.

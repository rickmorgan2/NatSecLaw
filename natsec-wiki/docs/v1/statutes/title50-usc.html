Title 50, U.S. Code: War and National Defense

Constitutional and code foundations
In force
U.S. Code Title 50

In force 50 U.S.C. 1 et seq.

The title of the U.S. Code that collects the laws on war powers, emergency powers, intelligence, and economic defense. Unlike Title 10 it has no single design; it is a shelf holding statutes passed over two centuries.

Key provisions

  • Wartime authorities: the Alien Enemies Act (sec. 21) and the Trading with the Enemy Act (sec. 4301).
  • War and emergency powers: the War Powers Resolution (sec. 1541), the National Emergencies Act (sec. 1601), and the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (sec. 1701).
  • Intelligence: the National Security Act (sec. 3001), covert action and oversight (secs. 3091-3093), the CIA Act (sec. 3501), and FISA (sec. 1801).
  • Economic defense: the Defense Production Act (sec. 4501), foreign investment review (sec. 4565), and export controls (sec. 4801).
  • Manpower: the Military Selective Service Act (sec. 3801).

Why it matters

‘Title 50’ is shorthand for intelligence authority, above all covert action under a presidential finding, in contrast to military authority under Title 10. The shorthand understates the title: it also holds the main checks on presidential war and emergency power. The core of its intelligence chapters is the National Security Act.

Current status

In force. It is not positive law: it is an editorial compilation of separate statutes, so the underlying acts control. Many sections were renumbered in 2013 and the former appendix was folded into the main title in 2015.

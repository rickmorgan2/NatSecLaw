Symington and Glenn Amendments

Defense and arms
In force
Targeted provision
U.S. Code Title 22

In force Targeted provision 22 U.S.C. 2799aa, 2799aa-1

Two nonproliferation amendments from 1976 and 1977 that cut off U.S. economic and military aid to countries that trade in nuclear enrichment or reprocessing technology outside international safeguards, or that test a nuclear device.

Read the law (official GovInfo text)

Current U.S. Code: 22 U.S.C. 2799aa, 2799aa-1

Key provisions

  • Symington (Sec. 101): aid cutoff for delivering or receiving unsafeguarded enrichment equipment or technology.
  • Glenn (Sec. 102): aid cutoff for reprocessing transfers, and mandatory sanctions when a non-nuclear-weapon state detonates a nuclear device.
  • Presidential waivers are available in narrow circumstances, and Congress has added country-specific waivers.

Why it matters

The Glenn sanctions were triggered against both India and Pakistan after their 1998 tests and then waived by legislation. They are the clearest case of aid conditionality used for nonproliferation, connecting the Foreign Assistance Act, the Arms Export Control Act, and the Atomic Energy Act.

Current status

In force as Sections 101 and 102 of the Arms Export Control Act, where they were moved from the Foreign Assistance Act in 1994.

Where it sits

  • Layer. Targeted provision. Single sections that grant a narrow authority, impose a condition, or require a report, often enacted in a defense authorization. Small in text, often decisive in practice. (How the layers work)
  • Builds on. Foreign Assistance Act, Arms Export Control Act.
  • Codified in. Title 22 U.S.C..
  • Era. Vietnam, Watergate, and reassertion (timeline).
  • Study. Unit 10: Arms transfers, export controls, and investment screening (core reading).

Sources

Everything this wiki draws on, with the official text of every statute first.

The wiki cites 266 distinct source links, 235 of them on official U.S. government sites. Official means a federal government domain (.gov or .mil): GovInfo, Congress.gov, the U.S. Code, the House Clerk, and members’ official House sites. Everything else, such as news reports, think tank analysis, and trackers, is marked secondary and is used for current status or for facts no official page states. The full list is also a plain file: all_sources.csv.

How statute links are built

Each statute page links to two official texts, both on GovInfo, the Government Publishing Office’s authenticated repository:

  • Enacted text. The public law as passed, for the 104th Congress (1995) onward, or otherwise the law as printed in the Statutes at Large. GovInfo holds every volume of the Statutes at Large back to 1789, so even the Militia Acts of 1792 link to their original printing.
  • Current U.S. Code. The section as codified today, from GovInfo’s edition of the Code. Where a law sits in a statutory note, the link opens the section that carries the note.

Links are built from each statute’s citation by the patterns in the GovInfo link service documentation, so they are only as accurate as the citation. Two kinds of entry have no citation of their own: the Constitution page links to Constitution Annotated on Congress.gov, and composite pages such as the annual defense authorization link to specific enacted laws listed in their sources.
